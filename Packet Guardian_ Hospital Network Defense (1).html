Packet Guardian: Hospital Network Defense

Keep a hospital’s network clean while infected packets try to get in.

Click a placed tower to upgrade or sell it.

Firewall blocks packets on dangerous ports: Telnet 23, SMB 445 and RDP 3389.

Filter inspects what’s inside packets. It stops malware disguised as normal traffic and opens encrypted tunnels, which split into the packets hidden inside.

Rate limiter slows all traffic passing through its zone so other towers get more shots, and drops DDoS flood packets several at a time.

Honeypot is a decoy server. Infected packets that pass near it get lured off the path and trapped, earning credits as intel. Normal traffic ignores it, because real users never touch a decoy. It can’t catch DDoS floods or ransomware.

Services are bought once. A backup server keeps offline copies, so when ransomware hits you can restore instead of paying, at the cost of your defenses pausing for 5 seconds. An email filter scans attachments and quarantines dangerous ones. A SIEM collects alerts in one log and forecasts the next wave.

DDoS attacks hit in waves 3 and 5: a flood of small orange packets. Each one does little damage, but the flood overloads the network and slows every tower’s firing. Rate limiters are the main defense. Firewalls help a little.

Stopping bad packets earns credits. Spend them on towers and upgrades (up to level 3). Click a packet to hit it by hand, but destroying a good packet is a false positive. Waves 4 and 6 end with a ransomware payload that every attacking tower can damage. If it reaches the network, it locks your files and demands a ransom: pay everything you have, even if that puts you in debt, or refuse and end the game. While you’re in debt you can’t build or upgrade.

Files on the left are what attackers want. Each breach corrupts one of them. Click a folder to open it and see what’s left. When all three are gone, the hospital goes offline.
